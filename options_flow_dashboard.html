Unusual Options Flow — Detection Pipeline

Big institutional money leaves footprints in the options tape before a stock moves, and a regular investor has no way to spot them in time. I built a two-step ML pipeline that watches ~53K real options prints and surfaces the small handful actually worth a look. Everything below is real data from Step 1 (Isolation Forest anomaly detection) on the cleaned set.

1

Why I built it

The best tell that something is about to happen in a stock is often a burst of unusual options activity. That signal exists in public data, but it is buried in tens of thousands of daily prints, so in practice only desks with tooling ever see it. I wanted to pull that edge out of the noise.

2

Who it's for

The active trader or analyst who wants to know where to look, not to be handed a trade. The job of the product is to shrink the haystack, and leave the judgment to a person.

3

The problem

About 27,000 cleaned prints a day. The few that matter are a needle in that stack, and scanning by hand means you miss them, or you drown. A plain rules filter throws away too much or too little.

Step 0 · Clean & engineer

Parse K/M premiums, filter 40+ ETFs, dedupe, then engineer 30+ features across moneyness, volume, premium, and implied vol.

Step 1 · Detect unusual flow

Isolation Forest (unsupervised) + Random Forest trained on repeat-flow labels surface anomalous trades in a low-label setting.

Step 2 · Predict success

Supervised model estimates which flagged flows clear >2.5% profit before expiry, using tracked outcomes as labels.

What stays human

Impact, risks & what stays human

Impact: this narrows ~27,000 cleaned prints down to ~1,300 worth a closer look, so a person reviews the tail that matters instead of the whole tape.

Honest limits: a flag is a prompt to investigate, not a trade signal, and false positives are expected; the Step-2 success model still needs labeled outcomes before it can be trusted; and this is decision support, not financial advice, a human makes every call.
