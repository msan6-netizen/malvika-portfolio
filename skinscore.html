SkinScore AI

I built SkinScore to answer the question a product label never will: is this actually right for me? It reads the ingredients, checks them against real evidence, and gives you a plain BUY, CAUTION, or SKIP, scored for your skin, not skin in general.

1

Why I built it

Skincare runs on marketing. Every label promises the world, and the one thing you actually want to know, "will this work for me?", is the one nobody answers. The scanners that exist grade a product's "hazard" in a vacuum. They can't tell you whether a serum will actually do anything, or whether it's quietly wrong for your skin, your medications, or your sensitivities. That gap is the whole reason SkinScore exists.

2

Who I built it for, and why them first

I started with the people a bad guess actually hurts: reactive or condition-prone skin, on medication, or juggling actives that fight each other. They already research everything and still end up unsure. Serving them first, instead of the casual shopper, was a deliberate call, it forces the product's bar up to trust and safety. Clear that bar, and it's easy for everyone else.

3

What 23 people actually told me

I didn't guess at the problem, I sat down with 23 people and listened. The same story came back again and again, in four acts. This loop, doubt to regret, is exactly what I set out to break.

1 · Doubt

"Is it safe, and will it work on my skin?"

Every purchase starts from uncertainty, no way to tell a real active from a nice-sounding one.

2 · Deception

Marketing traps

"Clinically proven, 90% success!", on a study of twenty people who look nothing like you. You end up feeling like a test subject.

3 · Damage

Trial, error, and harm

So you research for hours, buy anyway, and it's close to a coin flip. Sometimes the wrong call means a breakout, a reaction, a painful return.

4 · Regret

Money down the drain

An expensive product, wrong for you, and the cash is gone. Repeat a few times and you stop trusting the whole category.

4

The bet, and the calls behind it

The product is simple to use: answer a short, adaptive interview, then scan any product and get an explainable BUY / CAUTION / SKIP scored against your profile. Under that simplicity were a few decisions I had to get right.

Adaptive interview

Instead of a long fixed form, the AI asks the next-most-useful question until it knows enough. Less effort for the user, more signal for the score.

Three-score verdict

Personal Fit (safe and effective for you), Ingredient Science (is the formulation actually backed up), and Real Users (how it went for people like you).

9-dimension risk

Irritation, comedogenic, fragrance, pH, conflicts, and more, with a floor rule so one serious risk can't be hidden behind a friendly average.

  • My first version let the AI just answer, and I killed it. It sounded confident and gave me a different answer every time I asked the same question. For something people act on with their skin and their money, "confident but inconsistent" is dangerous. So I took the verdict out of the model's hands entirely: the score is now computed by fixed rules, same inputs, same answer, every time, and I can show exactly why. The AI only does the fuzzy work around it (running the interview, reading messy ingredient lists), never the score itself. I gave up flexibility on purpose to buy trust.
  • Evidence, not opinion. Every claim is tied to real sources (PubMed, CIR, SCCS) with citations, so a verdict can be defended, not just believed.
  • One rule I won't break. A single high risk can't be averaged into a friendly overall score. If something could hurt you, the product says so instead of smoothing it over.

How I know it's actually working

  • North Star: someone reaches a verdict they trust enough to act on. That's the whole point, so that's what I optimize for.
  • What I watch: how many people finish the interview, how fast they get to a verdict, and how often a scan returns a confident, sourced answer instead of a shrug.
  • How I keep it safe: a 30+ case test runs on every change and has already caught and closed 5 real safety gaps. Then I threw 21 adversarial attacks at the safety rules, trying to trick it into an unsafe "BUY", and none got through. Because the core is deterministic, any changed verdict is a genuine regression, never random noise.
6

What's next, and what stays human

  • Where it's going. A production-grade rebuild is underway on the revamp branch: a full PRD, competitive analysis, an eval-harness strategy, and a security and privacy pass.
  • What I keep human. Anything medical, a real reaction, a condition, a prescription, routes to a professional. SkinScore helps you make a purchase decision; it doesn't play dermatologist.
  • The risk I'm honest about. The evidence base has to stay current. A stale ingredient entry becomes a confident wrong answer, so it needs an owner and a refresh routine, not a "set it and forget it."
Under the hood (for the engineers in the room)

Proof it's real, not the headline. A React SPA on the front, Supabase (Postgres + Deno edge functions) on the back, and a Claude-powered layer grounded by an evidence-retrieval pipeline (a curated database plus live PubMed lookups).

React SPA (Vite)
   |  supabase-js (auth + function calls)
   v
Supabase Edge Functions (Deno)
   |- interview-start / next / complete   adaptive interview + profile synthesis (Claude)
   |- product-scan                        INCI ingredient parsing + product matching
   |- product-score                       core engine: 3 scores + 9-dim risk + claim verification
   |- ingredient-lookup                   evidence: DB -> fuzzy match -> PubMed + Claude
   v
PostgreSQL  (profiles, products, ingredient_evidence, reviews - all RLS-scoped)
FrontendReact 18 + TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind, shadcn/ui, TanStack Query, Zod BackendSupabase — PostgreSQL (RLS) + Deno serverless edge functions AI / LLMClaude API for interview, ingredient parsing, and enrichment RetrievalDB + fuzzy match + live PubMed (E-utilities), grounded in real citations (PMIDs) Data sourcesPubMed/PMC, CIR, EU SCCS/CosIng, Open Beauty Facts

Honest scope: the frontend was scaffolded with an AI app-builder and customized; the backend, scoring engine, adaptive interview, evidence pipeline, and data model are the hand-architected core, built with Claude as an engineering collaborator.
