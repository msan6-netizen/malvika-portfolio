Graduate capstone · AI product concept & prototype

Adaptive-ROI

An AI platform concept that helps schools and universities turn student and faculty sentiment into better decisions, and gives every student a personalized tutor, with humans kept firmly in the loop.

Academic concept & interactive prototype — MSIS 524, built end to end by me. Not a production system (see "what production would add" below).

▶ Narrated walkthrough of the interactive Figma prototype (2:45): the full closed loop from a student's confusion to a verified, ROI-linked credential.

Why now

In 2026 New York City, the nation's largest district, placed a one-year moratorium on student-facing generative AI for roughly 600,000 K-8 students. The worry is real: over-reliance and thinning critical thinking. But an outright ban also forfeits the upside, the way calculators once did, and the harder product question is not "ban or allow" but "how do we use AI with a human in the loop?" Adaptive-ROI takes that second path: AI as a force multiplier that lightens overloaded teachers' load and personalizes learning, never a replacement for the teacher.

The problem

A classroom moves at the pace of the average student, so advanced learners are held back and struggling learners fall behind; teachers are overloaded and can't personalize; and institutions have almost no reliable signal on student sentiment or on the real return of a program until it's too late to adjust.

The AI at the core

GenAIPersonalized tutor

Students can revisit a recorded lecture and ask the assistant to re-explain a concept a different way, dig deeper if they're ahead, or get more scaffolding if they're behind, catering to both ends a teacher can't reach at once.

GenAISentiment & signal

Captures and analyzes student and faculty sentiment from forums, chat, and recordings, surfacing where a curriculum or service is working and where it isn't, so decisions are evidence-based.

Human in the loopTeacher force-multiplier

AI drafts and assists (e.g., consistent first-pass grading and feedback); the teacher reviews and decides. The goal is to give teachers time back, not to remove their judgment.

Reasoned impact: if the tutor resolves the average confusion point in the moment, it frees teacher time for the students who genuinely need a human, and it gives leadership a sentiment signal weeks earlier than an end-of-term survey, while there's still time to adjust.

The broader platform vision

The full concept extends the AI core with two complementary layers, shown in the walkthrough:

Augmented realityImmersive learning

AR overlays to deepen engagement and comprehension for topics that benefit from spatial, hands-on interaction, and to generate richer learning-signal data.

BlockchainPortable credentials

Tamper-evident micro-credentials tied to real outcomes (skills, placements), giving students verifiable, lifelong proof of what they achieved.

Product thinking — risks & what stays human

  • Privacy is the first constraint. Student data is FERPA/GDPR-sensitive; sentiment capture needs explicit consent, tight scoping, and clear governance over who sees what.
  • Bias & academic integrity. Model outputs can be biased and can enable ghostwriting; the design keeps AI as an explainer and assistant, with the teacher as the grader of record.
  • Equity of access. AR hardware and devices aren't universal; anything that requires them risks widening gaps, so the AI tutor works on ordinary devices first.
  • Blockchain, honestly. Immutability is a double-edged sword (what about errors or sensitive records?), so it's scoped to verifiable credentials, not everything.
  • What production would add. As a course concept it has no evals or guardrails yet; a real build would need an evaluation harness for the tutor's answers, hallucination and safety guardrails, and human-review gates on anything that affects a student's record.
