Graduate capstone · AI product concept & prototype

Adaptive-ROI

An AI platform concept that gives every student a personalized tutor and turns classroom sentiment into decisions leaders can act on, with humans kept firmly in the loop. I took it from problem to a narrated, interactive prototype.

Academic concept & interactive prototype — MSIS 524, built end to end by me. Not a production system (I'm honest about that in Stage 6).

▶ Narrated walkthrough of the interactive Figma prototype (2:45): the full loop, from a student's confusion to a verified, outcome-linked credential.

1

Why now

In 2026, New York City, the country's largest district, put a one-year moratorium on student-facing generative AI for roughly 600,000 K-8 students. The worry is fair: over-reliance, thinning critical thinking. But a flat ban also throws away the upside, the way schools once did with calculators. To me the real product question isn't "ban or allow", it's "how do we use AI with a human in the loop?" Adaptive-ROI is my answer to that second question: AI as a force multiplier for overloaded teachers, never a replacement for them.
2

Who it's for

Two users who rarely get served at once: the student who's either bored because the class moves too slow or lost because it moves too fast, and the teacher who can see it happening but can't be in thirty places at once. Design for both, and you also give the institution a signal it's never really had.

3

The problem

A classroom moves at the pace of the average student, so the advanced ones coast and the struggling ones quietly fall behind. Teachers are overloaded and can't personalize for everyone. And leadership has almost no reliable read on how students actually feel, or on whether a program is working, until the end-of-term survey, when it's far too late to adjust.

4

The bet, and the calls behind it

Three pieces, deliberately kept in balance so the AI assists and never overrides the teacher.

GenAIPersonalized tutor

A student can revisit a recorded lecture and ask the assistant to explain a concept another way, go deeper if they're ahead, or add scaffolding if they're behind, reaching both ends a teacher can't cover at once.

GenAISentiment signal

Reads student and faculty sentiment from forums, chat, and recordings, and surfaces where a course is landing and where it isn't, so decisions rest on evidence, not the loudest voice.

Human in the loopTeacher multiplier

AI drafts a consistent first-pass at grading and feedback; the teacher reviews and decides. The point is to give teachers time back, not to take their judgment away.

The full concept extends this with two optional layers shown in the walkthrough, and I was deliberate about scoping them: AR for topics that genuinely benefit from spatial, hands-on learning, and tamper-evident micro-credentials tied to real outcomes. Both are add-ons, not the core, so the product still works on an ordinary laptop.

5

How I'd measure it

It's a concept, so I didn't invent numbers. Instead I defined what success would have to look like, and it isn't "time spent in the app."

North Star
Students unstuck, in the moment
Teacher
Hours of grading returned
Early warning
At-risk students flagged sooner
Signal
Sentiment, weeks before term-end

The one that matters most: catching a struggling or disengaged student while there's still time to help, instead of reading it in a survey after the grade is locked in.

6

What's next, and what stays human

  • Privacy is the first constraint, not an afterthought. Student data is FERPA/GDPR-sensitive; sentiment capture needs explicit consent, tight scoping, and clear rules about who sees what.
  • Bias and academic integrity. Models can be biased and can enable ghostwriting, so the design keeps AI as an explainer and assistant, with the teacher as the grader of record.
  • Equity of access. AR hardware isn't universal, so anything that needs it stays optional; the tutor works on ordinary devices first.
  • Blockchain, honestly. Immutability cuts both ways, so I scoped it to verifiable credentials only, not everything.
  • What a real build would add. As a course concept it has no evals or guardrails yet. Production would need an evaluation harness for the tutor's answers, hallucination and safety guardrails, and human-review gates on anything that touches a student's record. I'd rather say that plainly than pretend it's finished.
