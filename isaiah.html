1

Why I built it

When something goes wrong with a phone or a TV, most of us just poke around until it works. For a lot of older adults, that same moment is genuinely frightening, they worry they've broken something expensive, they feel foolish, and their options are to wait on a busy family member or give up on the task entirely. I wanted to build the patient, never-rushed helper that meets them right there, before the fear turns into "I just won't use it."

2

Who I built it for

Older adults facing everyday technology, the users most products quietly design *around* rather than *for*. Designing for someone anxious and non-technical raised the bar on everything: the pace, the language, the layout, even the choice to make this page warm and calm instead of a dark, techy dashboard. That's a deliberate call, not an accident.

3

The real problem is emotional before it's technical

The thing I kept coming back to: the blocker usually isn't the router, it's the panic. Solve the feeling first and the fix becomes easy. So the whole product is built to remove four very human failure points before it ever gives a technical step.

Panic

"Did I break it?"

The fear of having done something irreversible freezes people before they even start.

Jargon

Words nobody explains

"Toggle the input source" means nothing. Every unexplained term is another reason to give up.

Overload

Too many steps at once

"Go to settings, then network, then reset" is three chances to lose the thread.

Embarrassment

Feeling like a burden

People stop asking for help long before they run out of problems.

4

The bet, and the calls behind it

Isaiah is a patient neural voice agent. He settles the person first, then gives exactly one small, physical step at a time and waits for a "done" before the next. A few decisions made him work rather than just talk.

  • A real neural voice, not the browser's. My first version used the browser's built-in speech. It came out robotic and different on every device, exactly the wrong feel for a nervous caller. So I moved to a single warm, consistent neural voice, because for this user, *how* it sounds is part of whether they trust it.
  • Grounded in real guides, never guessing. He answers from verified device and support material, not off the top of the model's head. If he doesn't know, he says so and runs a safe reset or hands off, guessing at a fix for a scared person is the one thing I won't allow.
  • A human is always one sentence away. Anything touching passwords, accounts, or money, or any caller who's still stuck or just wants a person, is handed to a human. On confusion, he escalates on his own rather than digging a deeper hole.
  • Voice first, but never voice only. If a mic isn't available, the same help works as text. The person is anxious enough without a dead end.
5

How I'd know it's working

For a support agent, the honest measure isn't call volume, it's whether people leave calmer and unstuck. So the metrics are built around resolution and reassurance, not engagement.

North Star
Issue resolved, caller calmer
Trust signal
Sentiment lift, start → end
Safety
Clean human hand-off rate

Every call is auto-scored afterward (did it resolve? did the caller's sentiment improve?), the same evaluation habit I built at Radiant, applied to a very different product. I'd watch containment (fixed without a human) balanced against appropriate escalation, because a good escalation is a success here, not a failure. For this prototype the human-transfer and callback tools are wired to lightweight mocks, so the full loop can be shown end to end without a paid telephony account.

6

What's next, and what stays human

  • Language, done right. He's multilingual (English, Spanish, and major Indian languages), but the non-English greetings still get a native-speaker review before I'd call them finished, close enough isn't good enough for someone's first impression.
  • What stays human. Reassurance, judgment, and anything touching accounts or payments. Isaiah removes the fear and the easy blockers; a person handles the rest.
Under the hood (for the engineers in the room)

Isaiah runs on ElevenLabs Agents: real-time speech in and out with one warm neural voice, multilingual, grounded (RAG) on real device-support material, with an automatic hand-off to a human. The human-transfer and callback-scheduling tools are backed by lightweight mock webhooks for this prototype, so no paid telephony or scheduling account is needed to demo the whole workflow. Post-call analysis scores each conversation for resolution and sentiment.
